# High Resistance vs Low Resistance Liquidity - ICT Concepts

## TL;DR
Defines low resistance liquidity (LRL) as failure swings: a swing high followed by a lower high (or low with a higher low) whose liquidity was never swept before the reversal. High resistance liquidity (HRL) is the opposite: liquidity already swept (higher high / lower low) before the reversal, leaving the remaining liquidity further away. Usage: target LRL and keep HRL on your side for the stop. On the daily, mapping where failure swings sit and where HRL just formed adds to bias. Examples on unnamed symbols, NQ daily, ES (SMT vs NQ), and order-block / mean-threshold entries with the stop at the HRL. Ancestor of the TTFM "failure swings get revisited, protected swings hold" logic.

## Definitions
- **Failure swing**: swing high with a lower high next to it (or low with a higher low), the first one never swept before the reversal (00:02-00:37).
- **Low resistance liquidity (LRL)**: failure swings; resting liquidity price can run easily; the target (00:37, 01:34).
- **High resistance liquidity (HRL)**: a swing that was just swept (higher high / lower low) before the reversal; remaining liquidity sits beyond it; the stop side (01:07-01:34).
- **Order block**: down-close candle(s) into an important level, validated by a close above them (08:24, 09:23, 09:54).
- **Mean threshold**: 50% of the order block; reaction / entry level (08:24, 09:23).
- **Propulsion block**: order block formed off a prior order block's reaction; its mean threshold holds (10:24, 10:54).
- **SMT**: one index makes a new high/low the other does not; trade the weaker (07:54).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Mark failure swings (LRL) on both sides on the daily and lower timeframe (04:24).
2. Find which side has just been swept with a close back inside (HRL) (04:54).
3. Bias toward the LRL side; HRL side should hold (05:24, 06:25).
4. Optional: SMT to pick the weaker market (07:54).
5. Wait for CISD / MSS or an order-block validation (close over down-close candles) (02:02, 09:23).
6. Enter at the order block or its mean threshold (discount), stop beyond the HRL, target the LRL (10:24).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: unnamed symbol, higher-timeframe failure swing (02:29-03:25)
- Stacked highs and a higher-timeframe failure swing above; equal lows just taken into a gap (HRL). Price runs the failure-swing highs, takes one high, leaves equal highs, retraces for another expansion.

### Example 2: unnamed symbol, daily -> 1h (03:25-04:24)
- Three days of untaken previous day lows = LRL; second day took the first day's high (HRL above). Hourly: range high taken (HRL), failure swings below; retrace into the range then drop through the LRL.

### Example 3: NQ 1D range (04:24-05:56)
- Low taken, then next day sweeps and closes back inside = HRL on the lows; failure swings on the highs -> long bias; price runs the high. Second case: high taken, failure swings on the low; consolidation, then the low runs; later a high with a lower high and a sweep in an uptrend -> price trades to the previous highs; a lower-timeframe PDL sweep created HRL first.

### Example 4: unnamed symbol, daily confluence (06:25-07:54)
- Failure-swing highs as draw; sweep of a low that is also the previous day low (HRL) -> anticipate it holds; price trades to the highs. Daily: last three days' highs lined up; day takes PDL and closes above 50% of the wick -> next day higher into highs and a FVG.

### Example 5: NQ high purge -> ES short (07:54-08:54)
- NQ purges a high (HRL); ES makes SMT (weaker). ES has an obvious series of lows (LRL) above a FVG with an order block. Price runs the lows into the order block; mean threshold reacts; then stacked lower highs are the target and are reached.

### Example 6: unnamed symbol, order-block long (08:54-11:23)
- PDL just taken; swing high with a lower swing high (failure swings) above. Close over down-close candles validates an order block, but it is not in discount -> use mean threshold or a second entry. Mean threshold holds, another order block validates; sweep of a low (HRL) and a down-close candle into the important level, close above -> entry, stop on the HRL, target the highs; reached. Why not trust the earlier low: price never closed over that level; after the sweep a new order block off the propulsion block forms and its mean threshold holds.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Entering an order block that is not in discount; use the mean threshold or wait (09:23).
- Using an unswept low that price never closed over as invalidation (10:54).

## Conflicts and open questions
- Failure swing here = LRL (unswept lower high); in TTFM videos "failure swings" are equal highs without a protected close. Similar but the definitions are not identical.
- "Important level" for an order block is not defined (08:24).
- Close above 50% of the wick (07:26) is a numeric clue but the wick reference (which candle) is not stated.
- How many failure swings make a valid LRL target is not stated.
