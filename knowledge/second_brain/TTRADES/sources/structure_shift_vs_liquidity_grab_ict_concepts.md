# Structure Shift vs Liquidity Grab - ICT Concepts

## TL;DR
Early ICT-era lesson on reading displacement and, more importantly, the lack of it. Displacement = large aggressive candles closing near their high/low, closing over structure. When price takes a high or low but closes straight back inside the range (no displacement), that is a liquidity grab and the other side of the range is the likely target; when it closes aggressively through, that is a structure shift and the move continues. He walks one ES 1m session from before the RTH open, marking each short-term high/low and asking "displace or fail?", and ties failed displacement to the broadening formation / power of three. Practical rule: in a bullish leg ("buy side of the curve") lows taken without displacement are long entries; in a bearish leg, highs taken without displacement are short entries. Pre-TTFM: no candle 2/3 or CISD vocabulary, but it is the precursor of "close through vs wick through" logic.

## Definitions
- **Displacement**: large aggressive candles closing in their high or low, through structure (00:07, 00:34).
- **Lack of displacement / failure to displace**: price trades beyond a high/low but closes straight back into the range, without big closes beyond (00:34, 02:43).
- **Buy side of the curve / sell side of the curve**: the current bullish or bearish leg of delivery; flipped by displacement through a higher-timeframe swing (01:35-02:09, 03:13).
- **Broadening formation / power of three**: failing one side of a range, then delivering to the other side; consolidation, manipulation, distribution (03:45-04:18).
- **BPR**: balanced price range; here, an aggressive break followed by an immediate aggressive move back in, seen on a lower timeframe (05:26).
- **Box setup**: retest of the previous high followed by displacement below an intermediate-term low (05:26); not further defined.

## Procedure / checklist
1. Mark the current short-term high and low inside the leg (01:03).
2. When price trades beyond one, judge the closes: big closes beyond = displacement (continue that way); immediate close back inside = failure (lean the other way) (02:09-02:43).
3. Prefer a higher-timeframe (intermediate) swing being broken before calling a leg flip (01:35, 05:26).
4. After a flip, look for an FVG in the displacement, ideally aligned with a breaker, as the entry; failure to trade back into it supports continuation (06:00).
5. Trend entries: in a bullish leg buy sweeps of lows that fail to displace; in a bearish leg sell sweeps of highs that fail to displace (07:09-07:37).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: ES 1m, one session around the RTH open, date not readable (01:03-07:37)
- Before RTH open price takes the previous day's high without displacing over it (01:03).
- Short-term lows are swept but not displaced below, so price goes higher (01:35).
- At RTH open, aggressive selling breaks a higher-timeframe low: displacement down, now sell side of the curve (01:35-02:09).
- Each new low is broken aggressively: continuation lower (02:09). A low breaks but price returns to the range; he notes an SMT with NQ there (02:43).
- Price displaces up over a high: buy side of the curve (03:13). Later a weak break of a low returns into the range = power of three, distribution up (03:45-04:18).
- Morning high taken; a short-term low breaks only into an FVG and returns, then a new high (04:51). That high breaks but immediately reverses (BPR) (05:26).
- Retest of the previous high, then aggressive displacement below the intermediate-term low with closes below structure (05:26-06:00).
- Entry idea: small FVG aligned with the breaker as a short; price struggles to re-enter it and continues lower, repeated failure-to-displace / move-lower cycles (06:00-07:09). No stop or target is given.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Many traders focus only on displacement and ignore what the lack of displacement tells them (00:07).
- Do not treat a break of a tiny short-term low as the structure shift; he waits for a higher-timeframe low (01:35).
- A break that closes right back into the range is "not really the displacement you want to see" (02:43).

## Conflicts and open questions
- "Large aggressive" candles and "closes in the high or the low" have no numeric threshold (body %, range vs average).
- "Higher timeframe low" vs "short-term low" vs "intermediate term low" is never defined by timeframe or swing order.
- How many closes beyond structure are needed? He sometimes says "one close immediately back into the range" fails (06:00) but never states the count required to confirm.
- BPR here is used for a break-and-reverse pattern, which differs from the usual ICT BPR (overlapping opposing FVGs); flag before mapping.
- "Box setup" is named but not defined (05:26).
- Title says "Structure Shift vs Liquidity Grab" but the speech says "displacement and the lack of displacement"; the two framings are treated here as the same.
- Chart frames show ES 1m (CME_MINI); the date is not readable.

## Glossary additions
- `buy side of the curve` / `sell side of the curve`: current bullish / bearish leg of delivery.
- `broadening formation`: here used as a synonym for power of three (fail one side, deliver the other).
- `BPR` (balanced price range): here an aggressive break immediately reversed back into range.
- `RTH open`: regular trading hours open (9:30 ET).
- Mishearings: "handles" = candles (00:34); "failed through this place" likely = "failed to displace" (02:43); "lower by side of the curve" likely = "lower, buy side of the curve" (05:26).
