# Jokerszn | Daily Orderflow - ICT Daily Bias

> Source: Whisper transcript (segments ~30 s, so times below are segment starts and can be up to ~30 s early). No frames exist for this video, so every level, price and date comes from speech only. The host (TTrades channel, unnamed in the audio) only introduces and closes; all teaching is by the guest, introduced as "David" (Jokerszn). Job listed 46 min; the transcript runs to 49:19+.

## TL;DR
Guest lesson (ICT vocabulary, not TTFM): David, mainly a EURUSD trader, builds his daily bias in four steps. (1) Pick a higher-timeframe draw using only two liquidity pools: old highs/lows (external) and daily/weekly FVGs (internal). (2) Wait for daily order flow to confirm the draw, read from how daily *closes* treat PD arrays (respected vs disrespected and inverted); wicks only take liquidity. (3) Forecast the next daily profile (expansion, retracement, consolidation) from the last three candles, extension vs average, HTF levels reached and range EQ; he prefers trading expansion days and avoids a fourth same-direction day. (4) Refine the daily PD array to an H1 POI and execute in the London or New York kill zone after a sweep of the opposing side and a lower-timeframe change in order flow, doing "the same thing" on M15/M5 that he does on the daily. Two EURUSD trade walk-throughs: an H1 short into PPI news and an M15 London short with a breaker add-on.

## Definitions
- **Daily order flow**: the direction shown by how daily closes treat PD arrays; bullish when bullish PD arrays hold and bearish ones are closed through and inverted (10:17).
- **Expansion profile**: one-sided daily price delivery; his preferred day to trade (05:26, 24:13).
- **Retracement profile**: a day that retraces into discount (bullish order flow) or premium (bearish) against the daily order flow (05:52, 24:42).
- **Consolidation profile**: a daily candle that opens and closes at almost the same price (08:19).
- **Daily swing high fractal**: a daily high with a lower daily high on each side (08:49).
- **Daily dealing range**: from the last daily swing low to swing high; 0.5 = EQ, above = premium, below = discount (08:49, 09:20).
- **Inversion PD array / inversion FVG**: an FVG closed through by a daily candle, then used as a level from the other side (10:45, 11:15).
- **Balanced price range (BPR)**: the overlap of a former bullish FVG and a new bearish FVG (or the reverse); also how he reads a large daily wick on lower timeframes (14:39, 15:10, 30:19).
- **Mean threshold (MT)**: the midpoint of a PD array; closing past it signals the array will likely fail (16:43, 17:12).
- **High-probability order block**: the last up-close (bearish OB) candle that swept buy-side before a close below a recent swing low / break in structure (01:35, 02:05, 22:42).
- **Stuck / trapped order flow**: price ranging between a bullish PD array below and bearish PD arrays above (26:12, 29:16).
- **Bullish / bearish trigger**: the daily close that ends the stuck state, e.g. close above the bearish OB (use it as a breaker) or close below the swing low and the FVG (26:12, 26:42).
- **Monday range**: Monday's high and low, marked every week as a time-based reference (20:46, 40:09).
- **AMD**: accumulation range, manipulation (sweep into a HTF PD array), distribution to range lows, then expansion (31:45).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Weekly and daily: mark old highs/lows and daily/weekly FVGs (plus aligned PD arrays such as volume imbalances and OBs); pick the draw (01:05-02:33, 03:59).
2. Wait for daily order flow to confirm the draw, judged on daily closes against PD arrays (02:33-03:31, 10:17-11:43, 22:12).
3. On reaching the objective: bias neutral, stay out until order flow shows intent (04:28-04:58).
4. After each daily close: list new and disrespected PD arrays; draw the daily dealing range and its 0.5 (09:20, 21:47).
5. Forecast tomorrow's profile: the last three candles, extension vs average expansion, HTF level reached, range EQ, Monday tendencies (06:21-07:17, 12:12-13:39, 18:36, 39:40).
6. Skip the day after a large expansion and the fourth same-direction day; skip trades at a range's EQ; trade range extremes only (12:42, 24:13, 27:41).
7. Pick the HTF POI (BPR, inversion FVG, OB, large-wick BPR) and refine it to H1 PD arrays in its near half (15:39, 30:19-32:15).
8. Pick the kill zone (London or New York, skip Asia), check the economic calendar for high-impact news (32:48-33:15).
9. In the kill zone: sweep of the opposing liquidity, then a lower-timeframe change in order flow (H1, M15 or M5; M1/M5 after news) (16:10, 35:25, 41:13-43:30).
10. Stop above the invalidation (London high / breaker / BPR), or wider at the daily wick EQ around news; first TP at session or Monday lows, partials toward the previous week's / weekly lows (34:57, 44:00-45:02).
11. When the next opposing PD array is closed through, move the first trade to breakeven and add a limit at the new breaker (46:05-47:09).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: EURUSD 1W/1D bias build, "last year" (01:05-12:12)
- Weekly high resting inside a weekly volume imbalance in premium, aligned with a weekly order block (last up-close candle; it or the next candle swept the prior weekly high, then a break in market structure down in later weeks) (01:05-02:05).
- Two weekly highs above = buy-side objectives, so bias bullish; daily order flow bullish; price closes above daily equal highs, next day expands into the weekly highs / volume imbalance (02:05-03:31).
- Objective reached: bias neutral. Six bullish daily candles, the last two large versus average, at the weekly level, so consolidation expected; two consolidation days follow (04:28-08:49).
- Daily swing high fractal forms; dealing range drawn with 0.5; price tags a daily FVG but holds above its MT (still bullish); a daily close below the FVG makes it a bearish inversion; target the lower FVG and the discount (08:49-11:43).

### Example 2: EURUSD 1D profile sequence and stuck order flow (12:12-30:19)
- Last three daily candles bearish (expansion, small expansion, bearish consolidation that formed the swing high), so expect consolidation; it comes (12:12-14:09).
- Price between a bullish FVG and the bearish inversion FVG; the new bearish daily FVG overlaps the inverted bullish one = bearish BPR (red box), the HTF POI for intraday sells (14:09-15:39).
- Price trades above the previous day's high into the BPR's lower half, expands down into the lower bullish FVG and closes below its MT, a warning it will fail (16:43-17:42).
- Consolidation into the discount at the range EQ; another BPR forms; weekly order flow still bullish so a higher low is possible (18:08-20:14).
- Price runs the swing high that is also the Monday high (H1: Monday London session high) into the bearish BPR and rejects; no close above the lower BPR (20:46-22:12).
- New high-probability bearish daily OB aligned with the bearish inversion FVG; next target an unmitigated daily FVG; no trade the day after the large expansion; a retracement profile against order flow leaves equal lows (22:42-25:15).
- The bullish FVG is the weekly "last defense" for a higher low; order flow is stuck between it and the OB/IFVG above. Triggers: daily close above the OB (bullish, use as breaker) or below the swing low and FVG (bearish, target the sell-side below the weekly swing low). Two inside days follow (25:15-27:41).
- Sell-side taken first into the daily FVG's MT, so leaning long toward the equal highs; then the buy-side is taken into the daily OB and rejected back to about the open, so he aims for sells (28:11-29:48).

### Example 3: EURUSD H1 short into PPI (30:19-36:30)
- The large daily wick is read as a BPR; inside its lower half an H1 bullish FVG failed and an H1 bearish FVG formed, giving an H1 BPR, plus an H1 FVG = intraday POI. AMD framing: range, manipulation above buy-side into a HTF PD array, distribution next (30:19-32:15).
- Skip Asia; PPI due in New York. London opens above the Asian highs; at the New York open price closes below the EQ of the BPR and not above the H1 FVG (32:48-33:54).
- Option on H1: sell with stop above the EQ of the daily wick (25 pip stop), TP1 "this low" for 2.8R, aggressive TP at the daily FVG lows for 3.5R; or wait for an M1/M5 entry after the news (34:57-35:25).
- Invalidation is a close above the H1 BPR, but the stop goes wider at the daily wick EQ because of news; the news sweeps London highs once more, price sells off and the final target is reached the next day (35:25-36:30).

### Example 4: EURUSD M15 London short with breaker add (37:01-48:29)
- Friday: daily close below the FVG's EQ (not the whole FVG); target the low of the daily dealing range. Five unswept previous-day highs above, so selling is low probability; possible intraday wick above them with a close below the IFVG EQ (37:01-38:36).
- Expect a bearish week with the high early. Monday is a retracement profile against daily order flow; equal highs below the daily FVG EQ; Monday high marked. Plan: wait for Tuesday London and an LTF change in order flow (39:10-41:13).
- Tuesday: Asia takes the previous day's high; London takes the Asian highs and the previous week's Thursday highs into the HTF POI (dotted line = EQ of the daily inversion) (41:13-41:52).
- M15 still bullish at first; M15 dealing range drawn; two small bullish FVGs (treated as one zone) are disrespected and an M15 bearish FVG forms = M15 BPR (42:23-44:00).
- Aggressive market short, stop above the London high (13 pips); conservative TP1 Asia lows (almost 2R); he aims for at least the Monday lows (3.6R), partials for the previous week's low and the weekly target (44:00-45:02).
- An M15 bullish OB inside the range is closed through, becoming a bearish breaker: stop to breakeven on trade 1, add a limit at the breaker low with the stop just above the breaker (no close above the breaker's EQ wanted); about another 3R; both hit the Monday lows (= Asian lows); remaining partials close at the daily lows by the end of New York (45:02-48:29).

## Mistakes and warnings
- Trading the fourth day after three same-direction daily closes (12:42, 14:09).
- Trading the day after a large expansion; it is usually retracement or consolidation (24:13).
- Trading at the EQ of a range: "one of the lowest probability setups or places in price" (27:41).
- Judging order flow from wicks instead of daily closes (22:12).
- Selling under a stack of unswept previous-day highs (38:06).
- Tight stops into high-impact news; use the wider structural stop (35:55).
- Trusting a PD array that has been closed past its mean threshold (16:43, 17:12).
