# Market Structure Shift vs Liquidity Grab - ICT Concepts

## TL;DR
Early TTrades ICT-concepts lesson: the difference between a market structure shift and a liquidity grab is displacement. Displacement = a large aggressive single or multi-candle move that closes over structure (usually leaving an FVG). A market structure shift = sweep then displacement closing through structure; a liquidity grab = failure to displace over structure (wick over, close back in). At every old high/low he applies a three-outcome read: displace out → retrace → continue; displace out → displace back in → reverse; or fail to displace → return into the range, usually to at least premium/discount of the range. Most of the video is a bar-by-bar 1-minute replay of that read; the end ties it to daily bias on swing points and stresses having a directional bias. This is the precursor of TTFM's close-based logic (C2 close inside vs C3 expansion, CISD).

## Definitions
- **Displacement**: large aggressive move, single or multiple candles, closing over structure; the FVG-creating candle is the focus (00:10-00:35).
- **Market structure shift (MSS)**: sweep, then displacement closing beyond structure, flipping trend (01:02).
- **Liquidity grab**: failure to displace over structure; price reaches over a high/low and closes back in (01:02).
- **Displacement met with displacement**: displacement out of a range answered immediately by displacement back (11:49).
- **Box setup**: referenced only by name; previous high used as resistance (08:58). Not defined here.

## Procedure / checklist
1. Mark the old high/low that price is returning to (01:32-01:59).
2. Watch candle closes at the level: does price displace out (aggressive close beyond) or fail to (close back in)? (01:59-02:28)
3. Fail to displace → expect a move back into the range; prefer it to reach at least premium (short) / discount (long) of the range (03:30, 07:03).
4. Displace out → watch the next candles: shallow retrace without displacement back in = continuation; aggressive displacement back in = reversal (04:35, 08:58).
5. Use respected / disrespected FVGs and order blocks to confirm which side is in control (02:28, 06:26).
6. When one draw is reached, look left for the next old high/low (11:49).
7. Only take setups in line with the directional (daily) bias (13:16-13:49).

## Mistakes and warnings
- Low timeframes show many structure breaks and failures; without a directional bias the read becomes overwhelming (13:16).
- A weak close over a high is not displacement; do not treat it as continuation (07:03).
- Do not call a reversal before displacement in the new direction (05:50).
