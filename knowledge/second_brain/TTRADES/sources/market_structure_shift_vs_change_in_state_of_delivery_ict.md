# Market Structure Shift vs Change In State Of Delivery - ICT

## TL;DR
Compares three signals of an order-flow change. Market structure shift (MSS): displacement and a close beyond a swing high/low (a change in trend). Change in state of delivery (CISD): a close beyond the opening price of the up-close (or down-close) candle series that ran into an important level, which validates that series as an order block. Inversion: a close fully through an FVG. CISD and inversion occur earlier than MSS, giving earlier entries (order block retest, its mean threshold, or an FVG) before expansion, but they also produce more losers where MSS never confirms. MSS entries wait for a retracement to a premium/discount array. He personally prefers order blocks (CISD) and tells viewers to backtest which suits them.

## Definitions
- **Market structure shift (MSS)**: displacement and close beyond a swing high/low; a change in trend (00:31).
- **Change in the state of delivery (CISD)**: close (with displacement) beyond the opening price of the opposite-colored candle series that ran into an important level; validates it as an order block (00:57).
- **Order block**: the up-close (bearish case) or down-close (bullish case) candle series validated by a CISD (00:57, 01:52).
- **Inversion**: a full close through an FVG, signalling changed order flow (05:42, 06:42).
- **Mean threshold (of an order block)**: its 0.5 level (04:18).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Price reaches an important level (old high, FVG, buy stops) (02:52, 06:11).
2. Mark three triggers: FVG (inversion), opening price of the candle series into the level (CISD), the swing (MSS) (06:11).
3. CISD-style entry: after the close beyond the opening price, enter on the OB retest / mean threshold / FVG; stop on the extreme (03:49).
4. MSS-style entry: after the close beyond the swing, wait for a retracement into a premium/discount array (07:45).
5. Backtest which trigger fits your system (08:50).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: identifying both, bullish (01:52-02:21)
- Price reaches a discount FVG and closes back inside the range. Next candle closes over the down-close candle (CISD) and over the swing high (MSS) together; order block retested before higher.

### Example 2: CISD earlier entry, bearish (02:52-04:50)
- Old high hit. Close below the up-close series (CISD, bearish, target old low) comes before the MSS (close below the lows). No retracement after MSS, so MSS gives no entry.
- CISD entry: OB retest with stop on high gives 1.1R; alternatives: FVG or OB 0.5 mean threshold; tagged in, TP hit.

### Example 3: CISD loser (04:50-05:42)
- CISD prints (close below up-close series) but no displacement/close below the old low; structure stays bullish; CISD short would lose; MSS trader would not short.

### Example 4: CL monthly / daily (05:42-08:21)
- Monthly buy-side liquidity; daily old high and buy stops raided. Close past 50% of the bullish FVG (not a full inversion for him); then full inversion plus CISD on the same candle (order block validated); OB retested next candle; new up-close candle; MSS only a day or two later. After MSS, price retraces into the premium order block and reacts; a new order block forms and is retested; continues lower.

## Q&A
- **Q (08:21):** Which is best: inversions, MSS or order blocks? **A:** Depends on what your eyes see best and confidence; he prefers order blocks; others use inversions or MSS; backtest.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Treating CISD's early entry as always better: it can put you in losers that MSS would filter (04:50-05:23).
- Calling a close past an FVG's 50% an inversion; he waits for a full close through (06:42).
- Waiting for MSS can mean no entry when price does not retrace (04:18).

## Conflicts and open questions
