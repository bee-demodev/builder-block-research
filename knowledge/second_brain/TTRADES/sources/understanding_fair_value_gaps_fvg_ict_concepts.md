# Understanding Fair Value Gaps (FVG) - ICT Concepts

## TL;DR
Definitional lesson on fair value gaps: a three-candle pattern where candle 1's low and candle 3's high (bearish) or candle 1's high and candle 3's low (bullish) do not overlap. Bearish FVG = SIBI, bullish FVG = BISI. He watches three levels inside an FVG (start, consequent encroachment / 50%, full fill) and judges respect or invalidation by candle closes relative to the CE and the FVG boundary. A failed FVG (closed through) inverts and can act as support/resistance later, not necessarily immediately. Ends with FVG vs volume imbalance vs gap. Instruments and timeframes are not named in the speech.

## Definitions
- **Fair value gap (FVG)**: three-candle pattern with no overlap between candle 1's and candle 3's wicks (00:10).
- **SIBI**: sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency; bearish FVG (02:37).
- **BISI**: buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency; bullish FVG (02:37).
- **Consequent encroachment (CE)**: 50% of the FVG (03:37).
- **FVG inversion**: failed FVG (closed through) later acting as opposite support/resistance (05:32).
- **Volume imbalance**: close-to-open gap between two candles with traded overlap of the wicks (08:50).
- **Gap**: no trading at all between two candles (08:50).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Check candle 1 vs candle 3 wicks for no overlap; mark the gap (00:40).
2. Classify as SIBI (bearish) or BISI (bullish) (02:37).
3. Mark start, CE, and far edge (03:37).
4. On return, judge by close: closes back out = respecting; close beyond CE = first warning; close through = invalid (04:09-05:04).
5. If invalidated, extend it forward; on a later return, look for it to act as inverse support/resistance (06:03).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: FVG identification (02:08-02:37)
- Two consecutive bullish FVGs, then an aggressive move the other way forming a bearish FVG. Instrument/timeframe not named.

### Example 2: SIBI/BISI respect and invalidation (04:36-05:32)
- SIBI: price reaches in, does not close over the CE (respected). Another SIBI is closed straight through (invalidated). BISI: reaches in, closes out, touches CE, closes outside, continues higher.

### Example 3: Inversion (06:31-08:50)
- SIBI closed through, then supports price before a move higher.
- BISI: close below CE (first clue), fails to displace back out, close below the FVG; on return the old BISI is resistance; price goes lower.
- SIBI respected repeatedly while sell-side liquidity is taken twice, then closed over; later its CE acts as support, and again later the old SIBI supports with displacement out.

### Example 4: Volume imbalance and gap (08:50-09:20)
- Close-to-open gap with wicks overlapping = volume imbalance; reaction on return. Complete gap with no trading = gap.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Calling overlapping candle 1 / candle 3 wicks an FVG (01:10).
- Holding an FVG that closes over its CE or through it: price often does not reverse (04:36).
- Expecting inversion only immediately; it can come later after consolidation (06:03).

## Conflicts and open questions
- Flexibility at the CE (04:09) vs "close over the consequent encroachment ... price is not respecting that" (04:36): close beyond CE is both tolerated and an invalidation signal; a rule engine needs one threshold (e.g. CE close = warning, far-edge close = invalid).
- Timeframe of the invalidating close is not specified.
- No minimum FVG size.

## Glossary additions
- `SIVI` (Whisper): SIBI.
- `SIBI` / `BISI`: bearish / bullish FVG.
- `CE`: consequent encroachment, 50% of FVG.
- `volume imbalance`: close-open gap between two candles with wick overlap.
- `this placement` (Whisper, 08:16): "displacement".
