# Internal & External Liquidity Model - ICT Concepts

## TL;DR
A three-timeframe trade model built on the internal (FVG) / external (swing high/low) liquidity cycle: price moves external to internal and back to external. Mark a higher-timeframe level or draw, drop one timeframe to see the internal/external move, then on the lowest timeframe require a kill zone, a stop raid (sweep), a market structure shift and an FVG entry, targeting a fixed 2R. Default pairing is daily / hourly / 5-minute (aligned with the daily candle's formation), but it scales up to monthly / daily / hourly or weekly / 4h / 15m for swing trades. Four NQ examples plus two higher-timeframe examples; all hit 2R.

## Definitions
- **Internal liquidity**: a fair value gap (00:35).
- **External liquidity**: swing highs (buy-side) and swing lows (sell-side), three-bar swing definition (01:03).
- **Fair value gap**: three-candle pattern where candle 1 and candle 3 wicks do not overlap, showing aggressive price action (00:35).
- **New day opening gap**: the hourly FVG in Example 1 is also the new day opening gap (03:22).
- **Low-resistance liquidity**: aligned previous-day lows (07:50).
- **Mean threshold**: midpoint of a candle (here an up-close candle) used as a POI (08:17).
- **Consequent encroachment**: midpoint of an FVG, an entry point (09:51).
- **Expansion into expansion**: displacement answered by displacement the other way; likely continuation of the second (09:51).
- **Market maker buy model**: named for the weekly/4h/15m example, not defined (13:57).

## Procedure / checklist
1. HTF (e.g. daily): mark the level or draw on liquidity (FVG at discount, swing, lined-up lows) (02:53, 07:50).
2. One timeframe lower (e.g. 1h): identify the external-to-internal or internal-to-external move toward that draw (03:54).
3. LTF (e.g. 5m): be in a kill zone (London / New York) (03:22, 08:50).
4. LTF: stop raid / sweep of a local high or low (01:55, 09:20).
5. LTF: market structure shift (displacement through the opposite swing) (04:23, 06:17).
6. LTF: enter on the FVG (start of FVG, CE, or premium of range) (04:23, 09:51).
7. Stop over opposing candles / FVG candle extreme; if messy, use the next higher timeframe structure (06:50, 07:21).
8. Target 2R; runners optional (04:49).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: NQ 1D / 1h / 5m, long (02:24-05:48)
- Daily: sweep of a swing low with an aggressive close; waits a few candles. Displacement up violates an FVG; FVG at discount of the range is the draw (02:53).
- Hourly: FVG (also the new day opening gap) is internal liquidity; reached in London; expected internal to external (03:22-03:54).
- 5m: reaches into hourly FVG, MSS up, two FVGs; entry at start of upper FVG, stop at lower FVG candle's low; target 2R at highs (3.16R was available to the daily FVG). Hit TP (04:23-05:20).
- Breakeven on runners would have been stopped before the daily FVG; full position with original stop would also have worked (05:20).

### Example 2: NQ 1h / 5m / 15m, short (05:48-07:50)
- Daily FVG just reached (important level); hourly high swept (external); lower hourly FVG is the draw.
- 5m: sweep of high, displacement below low (MSS), FVG. 5m messy, so 15m: balanced, small inversion; stop above inversion. Alternative stop at FVG candle's high. Both hit 2R.

### Example 3: NQ 1D / 1h / 4h / 5m, short (07:50-10:54)
- Daily: Thursday swept Wednesday's high, Friday reversed closing below its open; previous-day lows lined up (low-resistance liquidity).
- POI: mean threshold of the up-close candle (premium) preferred over its opening price. London consolidates; New York reaches the mean threshold.
- Hourly messy, 4h shows FVG (internal) with previous-day lows below (target).
- 5m: sweep of the 8:30 high, large FVG on displacement down (MSS); entry at CE, stop on the high, 2R hit; lows ran later.

### Example 4: NQ 1M / 1D / 1h, swing long (10:54-12:58)
- Monthly: previous month's low taken. Daily: equal lows created and run; "megaphone pattern" (future video); external to internal; at discount.
- Hourly: MSS then FVG entry, stop on the low, 2R at highs below the FVG; drawdown then TP.

### Example 5: NQ 1W / 4h / 15m, long (12:58-15:57)
- Weekly FVG (internal) expected to reach a high (external). 4h: reaches external, then internal retracement; he skips that counter-bias retracement.
- 15m: "market maker buy model"; MSS; prefers a second FVG nearer discount and aligned with breaker bodies; stop below low / down-close candles; 2R slightly above the high; TP hit; continues higher.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Expecting a retracement immediately after a very aggressive close (02:24).
- Moving stop to breakeven on runners can cut a trade before the HTF draw (05:20).
- Trading the retracement against HTF bias instead of waiting to trade the expansion out of it (13:26).
- Placing stops inside messy price action; go up a timeframe to see structure (06:50, 08:50).

## Conflicts and open questions
- "Kill zone" times are not defined in this video (only London / New York named).
- Stop raid / sweep size and MSS definition (which swing) are left visual.
- The 2R target is fixed; no rule for when to hold runners.
- Example 2 ("let this play out and see if we do reach that hourly fair value gap") ends without stating the outcome.
- Choosing between opening price and mean threshold is by premium/discount, but no general rule beyond this example.
- "Market maker buy model" and "megaphone pattern" named without definition.

## Glossary additions
- `stop raid` (Whisper: "stop rate"): a sweep of resting stops (liquidity sweep).
- `2R` (Whisper: "to R"): two times risk target.
- `draw on liquidity` (Whisper: "drawn liquidity").
- `Campbell's low` (Whisper, 04:23): likely "candle's low".
- `mean threshold`: 50% of a candle's range.
- `consequent encroachment` (CE): 50% of an FVG.
- `low resistance liquidity`: aligned/equal previous lows that price runs easily.
