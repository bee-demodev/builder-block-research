# Low Resistance Liquidity Run - ICT Concepts

## TL;DR
Defines low resistance liquidity (LRL) as a high/low with a failure swing (a later leg reversed without sweeping it, leaving liquidity resting) and high resistance liquidity (HRL) as a high/low already swept before the reversal. Before the session he trades, he marks LRL as the target because price is much more likely to expand through it. Multi-day runs with no previous day high broken = a zone of LRL on lower timeframes that price expands through once it starts. When targeting a swept (HRL) low, use the CE of its wick or the failure swing right after it instead of the low itself. Examples: NQ 15m bearish continuation through LRL into New York, GBPUSD daily/4h consecutive unbroken highs, NQ 5m/1m exiting at the failure swing rather than waiting for the old HRL low.

## Definitions
- **Low resistance liquidity (LRL)**: high/low with a failure swing; liquidity untouched (00:37, 01:07).
- **High resistance liquidity (HRL)**: high/low already swept before reversing; remaining liquidity sits higher/lower (01:07).
- **Failure swing**: later swing that fails to take the prior high/low before reversing (01:07, 03:06).
- **Consecutive unbroken highs**: run of daily candles none of which takes the previous day high; a lower-timeframe LRL zone (04:10-04:31).
- **CE of the wick**: 50% of the swept candle's wick, used as a target in place of a HRL low (05:28, 06:24).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Before the session, mark LRL (failure swings) on the higher timeframe and the execution timeframe (01:32).
2. Define order flow; for continuation, wait for premium + PD array (FVG) (01:32).
3. Target the LRL first, the draw on liquidity beyond (03:06).
4. If the obvious target is HRL, use the failure swing above it or the CE of its wick (05:28).
5. Exit at the failure swing / equal lows rather than holding for HRL (08:28).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: NQ 15m bearish continuation (01:32-04:10)
- Bearish order flow; wants premium of the range and a bearish FVG; failure-swing highs (LRL) above get taken, close back into the range -> sell-side target. Among stacked lows the LRL is reached, then a further low. Near New York, a new low that is not retaken = LRL; 15m FVG then drop through the LRL to the draw on liquidity.

### Example 2: GBPUSD 1D / 4h consecutive unbroken highs (04:10-06:53)
- Multiple days with no PDH taken; on 4h all previous day highs remain = LRL; price expands through them. Then fails to close out of the range after taking PDH -> bearish order flow ("where we're at today"). A sell-side low is swept and the next candles close back into range -> HRL; failure-swing highs above = LRL -> expansion through the range. On return toward the HRL low, uses the CE of the wick / failure swing after the low as the target.

### Example 3: NQ 5m / 1m short (06:53-08:28)
- Original consolidation took a low before going up (HRL); equal low present. 5m consolidation; move up and back into range = manipulation. 1m shows multiple failure swings (LRL); expects expansion through them, ideally the old low but more confident on the failure swing / equal lows. Price hits the failure swing and equal lows, consolidates a long time, then takes the old low -> would rather exit at the failure swing.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Targeting HRL lows directly; they are harder to break; use the failure swing or CE of the wick (06:24).
- Holding for an old HRL low costs time; exit at the failure swing / equal lows (08:28).

## Conflicts and open questions
- "Multiple consecutive days" (04:10) has no minimum count.
- "Where we're at today" (04:56) dates the GBPUSD example to the recording date, which is not shown in speech.
- Which wick's CE (the sweep candle) when several candles closed back inside is not explicit (05:28).

## Glossary additions
- `CE of the WIC` (05:28): consequent encroachment (50%) of the wick.
- `flow` (07:53, "reaching for this flow"): "low".
- `LRL run`: expansion through a cluster of failure swings.
