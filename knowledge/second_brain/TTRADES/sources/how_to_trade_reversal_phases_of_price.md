# How To Trade Reversal - Phases Of Price

## TL;DR
Part 4 of a 5-part phases-of-price series (expansion, retracement, consolidation, reversal, then a blending video). A reversal is "expansion met with expansion": price expands into a relevant level and the next phase is an opposing expansion (displacement back into the range) rather than a retracement or consolidation (the continuation signatures). Reversals should form at relevant swing highs/lows, most simply previous day / previous week highs and lows; reversals off random highs that leave failure swings or equal highs behind are suspect. On the lower timeframe the reversal shows as a CISD and forms the higher-timeframe wick (candle 2); his model then trades the continuation that follows (candle 3) from a protected swing, so the exact top/bottom need not be caught. After each expansion, read the next candles: retracement / consolidation = continuation, opposing expansion = reversal; when unclear, let another candle close.

## Definitions
- **Reversal**: expansion met with (opposing) expansion; forms the higher-timeframe wick (00:33, 10:02).
- **Continuation signature**: expansion-retracement-expansion or expansion-consolidation-manipulation-expansion (00:42, 13:17).
- **V-shaped reversal**: clean expansion into a level met with clean opposing expansion (01:48).
- **Relevant high/low**: a level price would logically reverse from without leaving obvious liquidity (failure swings, equal highs, previous day high) behind; previous day/week highs and lows are always relevant (02:50, 10:38).
- **Expansion**: visible as fair value gaps being created (11:27).
- **T-spot** (09:16): named once in the second example's next day ("we have the T-spot. This is a nice shallow move"), not defined.

## Procedure / checklist
1. Mark relevant highs/lows (previous day / week; swing points that don't leave nearer liquidity behind) (02:50).
2. When price expands into one, watch the next phase on the lower timeframe: retracement or consolidation = continuation; opposing expansion = reversal (01:22, 03:16).
3. Unclear candle → let another close (04:11, 05:45).
4. Confirm with a CISD (close through the series from the relevant swing); ideally a V-shape that sweeps the level (08:30, 14:10).
5. Prefer reversals paired with a daily candle closure (15:43).
6. Trade the continuation after the reversal: shallow retracement, protected swing, close through series → entry (09:16).

- Consolidation high taken; expansion met with expansion bearish, but off a random high leaving failure swing highs → not trusted. Next candles displace back into the range → bullish (expansion met with expansion again).
- Price expands through the relevant highs on the left; expansion met with expansion + CISD → bearish; large wick, should respect the mean threshold. - 10 a.m. spike; body expansion-retracement-expansion; reaction from a higher-timeframe FVG looked like retracement; then expansion lower.

### Example 2: daily reversal with large wick, ideal V-shape (07:34-10:19)
- HTF reversal candle with large wick = LTF expansion up met with expansion down.
- Expansion into a low sweeping lows, displacement back in, close over down-close series = ideal structure (traps early longs and breakout shorts).
- Next day: shallow retracement ("T-spot"), protected swing via close through down-close candles → expansion higher; repeated: retracement and expansion higher (same example as the retracement video).

### Example 3: candle 2 closure with V-shape CISD (10:19-12:30)
- Candle 3 continuation higher, but stopping at a high that leaves previous day highs → no reversal there.
- Price takes the high/target and equal highs, forms CISD → anticipate trade back into range, through the daily open, to internal liquidity (FVG); expansion lower followed.

### Example 4: previous day low swept (12:30-14:38)
- Expansion down (FVG) met with expansion up = reversal; close back inside previous range → bullish.
- Model prints CISD with V-shape; relevant low (not the nearer swing) is the POI; low taken = retracement; new CISD using the series from the swing high that created the swing low → expansion higher.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Pattern-trading a reversal off a random high that leaves failure swings: "why would I lose?" (03:44).
- Judging a single FVG candle as a reversal (05:45).
- Trading against the trend before a relevant high is hit (11:15).
- Reversal not aligned with a daily swing point / closure: likely to fail (15:13).
