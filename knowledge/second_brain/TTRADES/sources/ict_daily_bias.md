# ICT Daily Bias

## TL;DR
Early, quick ICT-era video (recorded "before I go on a trip") giving a seven-item checklist for daily bias, shown as an on-screen list over an ES daily chart: previous liquidity taken, swing highs/lows, relative equal highs/lows, market structure shift, imbalances (FVG and OB), discount or premium, and the HH/LH/HL/LL sequence. Bias = where liquidity is easiest to reach: after sell-side is taken, expect distribution higher toward resting buy-side (here relative equal highs near 4,300). He separates overall daily bias (bullish to 4,300) from a short-term lower-timeframe bias (bearish first, to grab liquidity below / fill an FVG). Pre-TTFM: no candle 2/3 or CISD vocabulary.

## Definitions
- **Previous liquidity taken**: the last swing high or low that was run; its side sets the expected distribution direction (00:07).
- **Liquidity**: stops resting above highs / below lows, especially relative equal highs and lows (00:38, 04:14).
- **Market structure shift (MSS)**: a short-term high (or low) broken with displacement, leaving an imbalance (FVG) (00:38).
- **Imbalances**: FVG and OB (on-screen checklist, frame 01:44).
- **Distribute**: deliver price away from the taken liquidity toward premium (after sell-side) or discount (after buy-side) (01:09).

## Procedure / checklist
On-screen list (frame at 01:44), in his order:
1. Previous liquidity taken? (00:07)
2. Swing highs / lows (market structure). (00:38)
3. Relative equal highs or lows (market structure). (00:38)
4. Market structure shift (MSS). (00:38)
5. Imbalances (FVG and OB). (01:09)
6. Discount or premium. (01:09)
7. Higher highs, lower highs, higher lows, lower lows. (01:09)
Then: reduce to the closest imbalances and liquidity, name the daily target, and decide whether a lower-timeframe counter move to nearer liquidity comes first (01:41-02:42).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: ES 1D -> 1h -> 15m, mid-2022 (chart shows 2022 axis), exact date not stated (01:09-04:44)
- Daily: downtrend of lower highs / lower lows; sell-side liquidity taken; MSS up with an FVG; a higher high put in (01:41).
- Target: "super equal high level" around 4,300 (01:41-02:10).
- Expectation: first a move down to grab liquidity under recent lows, possibly into the FVG, then higher to 4,300 (02:10).
- 1h: relative equal lows with liquidity under them = near-term target "for the week" before switching bullish (02:42).
- 15m: relative equal lows taken, move up through relative equal highs, then lower; he wants continuation down to "40.70" [4,070] before longs, then back to 4,300 (03:45-04:14).
- No entry, stop or outcome is shown.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Confusing daily bias with lower-timeframe direction: he can be bullish overall but bearish intraday until liquidity is taken (04:44).

## Conflicts and open questions
- "Relative equal" highs/lows have no tolerance defined.
- No rule for which liquidity pool is "closest" or "easiest" when several exist.
- Order of checks is given but no weighting; how conflicts between items (e.g. trend down but MSS up) resolve is only shown by example.
- "40.70" in the transcript is almost certainly 4,070 on ES; the price is not readable on the frame to confirm.
- He labels this "daily bias" but the target is described as "for the week" on the 1h (02:42); horizon is loose.

## Glossary additions
- `MSS` (market structure shift): short-term swing broken with displacement plus an imbalance.
- `relative equal highs/lows` (REH/REL, "super equal high"): clustered highs/lows where stops pool.
- `distribute`: deliver price away from the swept pool.
- Mishearings: "pick liquidity up here" = "take liquidity up here" (00:07); "min balances" = imbalances (03:45); "40.70" = 4,070 (03:45); "my pin" = my pen (02:10).
