# Internal & External Liquidity (Daily Bias) - ICT Concepts

## TL;DR
Internal range liquidity (IRL) is a fair value gap; external range liquidity (ERL) is a swing high/low (buy-side / sell-side liquidity). The market only does two things: reach for old highs/lows or rebalance an FVG, so price alternates ERL to IRL to ERL. Reading that cycle on a higher timeframe (e.g. weekly) gives directional bias / narrative for the lower timeframe, which is then used to find an entry (5m intermediate high, 1m order block; 15m CISD order block). Examples: ES weekly, gold hourly-to-1m short (3.23R), GBPJPY weekly/4h/15m market maker buy model, and NQ 5m showing it works on any timeframe. He credits "the MMXM trader".

## Definitions
- **Internal range liquidity (IRL)**: a fair value gap (00:10).
- **External range liquidity (ERL)**: swing high / swing low, i.e. buy-side or sell-side liquidity (00:39).
- **Inversion**: displacement through an FVG, flipping bias (03:18).
- **Intermediate-term high**: high formed in the 5m FVG used before the 1m entry (05:52).
- **Market maker buy/sell model**: named, not defined (07:23, 09:03).

## Procedure / checklist
1. On the HTF, identify the last ERL taken (or failed displacement) and the IRL (FVG) price is in or heading to (01:28, 04:13).
2. Bias = toward the next leg of the cycle (ERL to IRL, or IRL to ERL) (00:58).
3. Wait for the session start (04:41).
4. Middle TF: price reaches the FVG; for confirmation, wait for a high/low to form inside and be respected (04:41).
5. LTF: sweep / displacement, CISD, order block or FVG entry; stop on the swept extreme (05:17, 08:30).
6. Target the HTF ERL; leave runners to the next-higher ERL if desired (05:17, 09:03).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: ES weekly (01:28-03:49)
- Fails to displace below an old low; reaches into the FVG (IRL); respects and reaches the old high (ERL). New FVG forms; price draws back in; fails to displace below the previous bar, closes back in; next candle higher. Later takes a low, displaces over an FVG (inversion), bias up to the old high; no retracement, continues higher.

### Example 2: GC 1h / 5m / 1m short (04:13-05:52)
- Hourly: respecting an FVG, target the old low (IRL to ERL).
- 5m: reach into FVG at the start of his session; displacement down; confirmation: high forms in the 5m FVG, respected.
- 1m: after liquidity taken, order block entry, stop on the high, target hourly lows: 3.23R.

### Example 3: GBPJPY 1W / 4h / 15m long (05:52-10:04)
- Weekly: old high taken (ERL); FVG below (IRL); next ERL marked above.
- 4h: displacement below the old high, retracement into an FVG, another leg down into the weekly IRL; CISD over a high (sell model to buy model). Swing retest not given; new 4h FVG becomes IRL, 4h ERL above.
- 15m: consolidation on the FVG, sweep below, CISD, order block entry, stop on swept low; target 4h ERL hit; runners to weekly old high also reached. 4h market maker buy model within a weekly one.

### Example 4: NQ 5m after the open (10:04-11:52)
- Open creates a range; aggressive move out and back in; successive ERL-IRL-ERL alternation narrated through the session, including legs with no FVG until one forms.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Expecting a perfect retracement after an inversion; sometimes price continues without it (03:18).
- Entering before the session starts (04:41).

## Conflicts and open questions
- "Start of my session" is not given as a clock time (04:41).
- "Daily bias" in the title, but the main bias example is weekly; the daily-bias rule (failure to displace below the previous bar and close back in) is referenced to another video (02:40).
- The order block entry candle choice ("last closed candle ... when price took this liquidity") is visual (05:17).
- No rule for which FVG to use when several exist, or what "respect" means (close vs wick).
