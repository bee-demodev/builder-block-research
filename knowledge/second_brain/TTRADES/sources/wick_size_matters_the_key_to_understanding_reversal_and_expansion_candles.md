# Wick Size Matters: The Key to Understanding Reversal and Expansion Candles

## TL;DR
TTrades classifies each higher-timeframe (HTF) candle by its "opposing run" (open to the wick extreme against the expected direction). A small wick made early in the candle (shallow opposing run) leaves time and range for expansion, so it supports expansion targets (EQ, liquidity, average candle range). A large wick made with most of the candle's time/range is a reversal candle: if traded at all, target only back to the opening price (daily open) or liquidity near it. His preferred play is to let the large-wick reversal candle close and trade the next candle (candle 3) when it shows a small wick plus CISD. This adds a wick-size / time filter on top of the candle 2 / candle 3 framework of the TTFM, applied fractally from 1D/1h down to 15m/1m.

## Definitions
- **Expansion candle**: small wicks on both sides, large body; high/low made early with little range, closes near its high/low (00:30, 01:43).
- **Reversal candle**: large wick, small body; uses most of its time and range forming the wick, then returns towards the open (01:00, 02:29).
- **Opposing run**: the move from the candle's opening price to the extreme against the trader's bias; its size = the wick (03:15).
- **Shallow (small) opposing run**: small wick made early in the candle; supports expansion (00:37).
- **Large opposing run**: wick that consumes about half or more of the candle's time and significant range; does not support expansion (04:00, 10:07).
- **Reversal to expansion candle**: a candle that reverses and expands in the same period (small early wick + CISD) (03:55, 05:31).
- **T-spot**: area the TTFM indicator marks where candle 3's wick is expected to form after a candle 2 closure (09:21, 23:19).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Pick the HTF candle of your timeframe pair (e.g. 1h for 5m, 4H for 15m, 15m for 1m, daily for hourly) (03:00, 21:40).
2. Mark the POI the reversal should come from (previous day / previous candle high or low, range high) (06:17, 17:06).
3. Watch the opposing run after the open: shallow and early, or large and time-consuming? (03:15, 17:53)
4. Require a CISD (plus SMT when trading away from an untaken failure swing) to confirm the wick (07:49, 16:52).
5. Shallow run + CISD + enough time left: trade the expansion; target EQ, liquidity, or average candle range (01:50, 08:25).
6. Large run: either target only the opening price / nearby liquidity, or (preferred) let the candle close and trade candle 3 with a small wick + CISD (02:29, 04:25, 05:05).
7. Late in the candle with no confirmation: wait for the next candle's open (18:25, 21:40).
8. Stop taking continuation entries after candle 3 / candle 4 (22:00).

## Mistakes and warnings
- Trading a wick sweep without CISD: "It doesn't work" (07:02).
- Expecting a full expansion after a large opposing run: too much range for open-low-high-expand; target the open instead (11:41).
- Holding for the intraday low when it is a protected swing; target the daily open (12:13).
- Shorting before the draw-on-liquidity highs are taken (13:45).
- Entering late in a candle that took too long to form its reversal (18:25, 21:40).
- Scalping a reversal off a target after a large candle is very counter-trend (26:00).
