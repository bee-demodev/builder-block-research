# Relative Strength & Weakness + SMT Divergence (Full Guide)

## TL;DR
TTrades teaches how to pick which of two correlated assets to trade: long the stronger, short the weaker. Three tools, in order: (1) SMT, a structural divergence at the same swing (higher low vs lower low); (2) when there is no SMT, structure "separation", meaning how far each asset's new swing sits from the prior extreme; (3) candle closures, especially the candles that create the CISD or the continuation, and the candle 2 closure at a swept level. Strength can rotate during a move, so it is re-checked continuously. It is a selection filter on top of a higher-timeframe framework (e.g. a bullish fractal model), not a standalone strategy. Published page dated 2026-02-28 (page).

## Definitions
- **Relative strength / weakness**: which of two correlated assets is leading in the expected direction (00:14; page).
- **SMT (divergence)**: "between correlated assets a difference in structure" at the same swing (00:41-00:46).
- **Structure / separation**: distance of the new swing from the previous extreme; used when both assets make the same structure (01:32-02:20).
- **Candle closures**: comparing how the same candle closes on both assets (bullish close over the body vs doji, closing through a CISD level vs not) (02:28-03:32).
- **Correlated assets**: markets that should share structure, e.g. S&P 500 and NASDAQ, gold futures and gold/GBP or gold/EUR; page adds gold and silver futures (03:49-03:56; page).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Have a higher-timeframe bias first (e.g. bullish fractal model, 11:28) (15:26).
2. Mark the same swing high/low on both correlated assets (04:07).
3. SMT present? The asset that fails to make the new extreme in the trade direction is the stronger for that direction (01:11).
4. No SMT? Compare separation from the prior extreme (02:11, 13:46).
5. Confirm with candle closures, especially the C2 at the swept level and the CISD / continuation candles (02:48, 08:40).
6. Trade the stronger asset for longs, the weaker for shorts; keep re-checking because strength rotates (01:22, 11:13).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: ES vs NQ (03:45-06:55)
- Same high marked on both: ES takes it out, NQ does not -> NQ lower high (weaker), ES higher high (stronger) (04:07-04:38).
- Closures: ES strong bullish candle closing over the body vs NQ doji/small range; next candle NQ takes the previous candle's low and prints an engulfing bearish candle while ES is a doji (05:03-05:40).
- Separation from the previous high: ES a few points away, NQ far away (05:54-06:21). Both drop; NQ drops substantially more ("an anomaly of a day") (06:42-06:55).

### Example 2: gold futures vs gold/GBP, bearish bias (07:01-09:03)
- Current high vs the high to the left: taken on one, not on the other -> SMT, gold/GBP weaker (07:14-07:32). Separation not useful at highs; at lows, one takes a low the other does not (another SMT) (07:37-07:51).
- Key closure: one candle closes below, validating the CISD; on the other asset the same candle cannot close below the level -> gold/GBP weaker, gold slightly stronger; both continue lower (07:59-08:25).
- Subtle case: one candle closes over its previous body, the other does not; that asset takes the high first (08:50-09:03).

### Example 3: bearish, multiple SMTs (09:05-11:25)
- Two SMTs at two highs on different timeframes, both pointing to the same weak asset (09:16-09:59). No upside separation; at the lows one asset takes a low and the other does not (10:12-10:22). Focus the weaker asset for shorts (10:29). Later the strength switches in a consolidation: one flat, the other with more bullish closures (10:54-11:13).

### Example 4: gold euro vs gold futures, bullish fractal model (11:26-12:56)
- No obvious SMT; separation from the lows shows XAUEUR further away -> stronger (11:42-12:26). XAUEUR also takes a high the other has not (12:28-12:36). Closures: gold futures sweeps the high and closes below; XAUEUR closes over the high and over its previous candle (12:44-12:53).

### Example 5: bullish, SMT at lows (12:58-15:05)
- SMT at a low (higher low vs lower low) and a second SMT at another low; the asset propped higher is stronger (13:06-13:28).
- Closures at the same low: both bearish, but the strong one cannot close below the down-close candle while the weak one closes below; next candle the strong one is bought back over the down-close candle (continuation) while the weak one does not reach its open (14:16-14:50). Trade the stronger; it expands and takes the high, the weaker catches up later (14:50-15:05).

## Mistakes and warnings
- Comparing assets that are not correlated (03:49).
- Assuming strength is fixed; it rotates, especially in consolidations (11:13; page "Strength Can Rotate").
- Trading an asset only because it looks stronger, without HTF bias and an entry model (15:26).

## Conflicts and open questions
- Separation has no numeric threshold (points, % of range, ATR); "decent separation" vs "almost equal lows" (01:56-02:07) is visual.
- Which swing to measure separation from is left open: "It doesn't really matter which low you choose ... You can use multiple" (12:08-12:14).
- Timeframes of the examples are not stated and the frames are too small to read.
- Example 2 speech: "We have taken out this high here. All we haven't on this side" (07:18): which pane is gold/GBP is not stated; the conclusion (gold/GBP weaker) is.
- How to weight conflicting signals (e.g. SMT says A, closures say B) is not defined; the page orders them: SMT primary, separation if no SMT, closures to confirm.

## Glossary additions
- `gold GBP` = XAUGBP (gold priced in pounds); `gold euro` = XAUEUR.
- `dogey` / `dogee` = doji.
- `propped higher / lower` = held further from (or closer to) the reference extreme.
- `separation` = distance between the new swing and the previous extreme (TTrades' term for structure-based strength when no SMT).
