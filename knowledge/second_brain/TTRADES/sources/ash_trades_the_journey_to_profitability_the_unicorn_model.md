# Ash Trades | The Journey To Profitability & The Unicorn Model

## TL;DR
Guest interview on the TTrades channel. Almost everything is said by the guest, Ash ("Ash Trades"); the host (presumably TTrades) introduces, asks two short questions and adds two comments. Ash tells a four-year path: signal services (year 1, losing), testing retail methods such as a 9/21/55 SMA crossover (year 2, break-even), annotating every ICT video (year 3), then profitability with one model (year 4), always funding trading from extra work, never from bill money. His model is ICT's "unicorn": daily bias from internal (FVG) vs external (swing high/low) liquidity, hourly areas of interest in the bias direction (FVG or swing), then on the 5m at London or New York open a stop hunt into the area, displacement through the breaker block, and an FVG aligned with the breaker. Limit entry at the start of the breaker, stop on the other side, fixed 2R, no break-even or trailing, under 1% risk, max 1 trade/day and 2/week. This is ICT material on the TTrades channel, not TTFM: there is no candle 2/3, CISD or protected-swing language.

Speaker key: "Ash" = guest; "Host" = the interviewer (assumed TTrades, never named in the transcript). Unless a row says Host, the rule is Ash's.

## Definitions
- **Unicorn (model / entry function)**: breaker block plus an FVG formed in the displacement through it, aligned with the breaker; named by ICT (50:35, 1:15:34).
- **Breaker block**: in the bearish case, the lowest down-close candle before the leg up that took liquidity; consecutive same-direction closes are all included (1:16:36). Mirror for bullish (implied from the NQ example, 1:29:03).
- **Stop hunt**: price running above a swing high (or below a swing low) before reversing; required before a breaker is valid (36:38, 1:16:05).
- **Swing high**: three candles where the middle has a higher high than the candles either side ("lower high, then a new high, and then a lower high") (1:16:05). **Swing low**: middle candle with higher lows on each side (1:28:21).
- **Displacement**: a move that closes through the breaker block; he equates it loosely with "market structure shift" and "clear and decisive displacement" (37:49, 1:17:12).
- **Internal liquidity**: FVGs. **External liquidity**: swing highs or swing lows (1:12:11).
- **Areas of interest**: hourly FVGs or swing points in the bias direction where the 5m entry function is searched (1:13:53).
- **Entry function**: the 5m trigger pattern (here, the unicorn) inside the model (1:15:34).
- **Model**: entry function framed by psychology and risk management (1:23:06).
- **Institutional swing points**: ICT core content month 5; two types, breaker block or failure swing (36:08).
- **London open**: 2 a.m. Eastern (1:14:24). **New York open**: 9:30 (35:06).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Daily: is price at internal liquidity (a daily FVG)? Then bias toward external liquidity (the opposing swing low/high) (1:12:11-1:13:21). Alternatively he reads bias from daily closes above previous-day highs (NQ example, 1:26:56).
2. Hourly: mark areas of interest in the bias direction: FVGs and swing highs (bearish) or swing lows (bullish) (1:13:53, 1:27:33).
3. Wait for the session open (London 2 a.m. ET or NY 9:30) and for price to reach an area of interest (1:14:59).
4. 5m: identify a three-candle swing point; wait for price to run it (stop hunt) into the area of interest (1:16:05, 1:28:21).
5. Mark the breaker block (last opposite-close candle(s) before the leg that took liquidity) (1:16:36).
6. Wait for a candle to close through the breaker (displacement) leaving an FVG aligned with the breaker (1:17:12-1:17:52).
7. Place a limit entry at the beginning of the breaker, stop on the other side of the breaker, TP at 2R (1:17:52, 1:19:07).
8. Do not manage: no break-even, no trailing, no trimming (1:19:38).
9. One trade per day max, two per week max, under 1% risk; after the trade, stop for the day (1:24:12-1:25:17).

## Worked examples
### Example 1: GBPUSD 1D -> 1h -> 5m, London open, Fri 5 Jan 2024 (1:12:47-1:22:34)
- Context: daily price at a daily bearish FVG, so bias bearish toward the swing low (1:12:47).
- Hourly: one bearish FVG and one swing high (buy-side liquidity) heading into London open (1:14:24).
- 5m: at London open price moves into the hourly bearish FVG (1:14:59). A three-candle swing high forms and is run (stop hunt) (1:16:05-1:16:36).
- Breaker marked; first push fails to close below it, a later candle closes through, leaving an FVG aligned with the breaker (1:17:12-1:17:52).
- Entry: limit short at the start of the breaker; stop above the breaker; 2R target (1:17:52). Frame at 1:23:39 shows the box labelled "BB + FVG", an "1HR FVG" line, and right-axis values 1.26747 (entry), 1.26832 (stop), 1.26577 (target); chart caption "5/1/2024", replay bar "Fri 05 Jan '24".
- Outcome: price barely touches the breaker and drops; retraces toward entry, then hits 2R (1:18:36-1:22:34). He notes a forex spread could have prevented the fill, his reason to trade futures (1:18:36).

### Example 2: NQ 1D -> 1h -> 5m, New York open, date not stated (1:26:24-1:31:12)
- Context: NQ daily bullish most of the year, closing above previous-day highs, "wanting expansion"; bullish bias (1:26:56).
- Hourly: a swing low inside a bullish FVG, plus another bullish FVG (1:27:33).
- 5m: the NY open candle runs below a swing low (stop hunt) into the hourly areas of interest (1:28:21).
- Displacement up through the breaker with an aligned FVG; limit long at the start of the breaker, stop below the breaker, 2R target (1:29:03-1:29:43).
- Outcome: filled on first touch, sits near entry, rallies, pulls back near break-even (a break-even stop would have been taken out), then hits 2R (1:29:43-1:31:12).

## Mistakes and warnings
- Trading with rent or last-dollar money makes you fearful or forced, leading to bad risk (07:21-11:24).
- Moving the stop to break-even or taking 0.5R gets you out of trades that later hit target; both examples demonstrate this (15:54, 1:21:57, 1:31:12).
- Chasing many concepts (order block, mean threshold, OTE, rejection block) after the first one fails; the speaker (likely the Host) says traders "get so lost" (45:24).
- Changing the model after a losing streak (1:07:37-1:08:48).
- Re-entering after a win because of the "cushion" (1:23:06).
- Oversizing: too large a win breeds euphoria, too large a loss can end your trading (1:24:43).
- Forex spread can block fills at the breaker; he prefers futures (1:18:36).
