# Why Your Continuations Fail (Order Blocks + CISD Explained)

## TL;DR
A filter lesson for continuation entries (a retracement into a POI followed by a CISD that protects the swing). The ideal continuation is a fast V-shaped recovery from a POI that closes through the series of opposing candles in 1-3 candles. He gives three reasons continuations fail: (1) price is really consolidating, so the "CISD" is a manipulation of the range; (2) the CISD closure also takes a short-term high/low (liquidity), which is where the opposite setup forms; (3) a higher-timeframe target has already been hit. The fix in each case is to wait one more candle / another continuation, or to stand aside. He ties it to the fractal model: frame entries while the higher-timeframe wick is forming, not after the HTF candle has already expanded. The companion page (2026-02-14) adds no rules beyond the video.

## Definitions
- **Continuation**: a retracement into a POI that is rejected with a CISD (closure through the series of opposing candles), protecting the swing and expected to continue the trend (00:34, 04:56).
- **Ideal continuation**: POI reach + V-shaped recovery + quick closure through opposing candles in 1-3 candles, without having taken the previous high yet (04:16-05:40, 09:55).
- **Consolidation (vs retracement)**: price fails to close back through the opposing candles, or does so slowly/sideways; treat range highs/lows as POIs (07:18, 09:01).
- **Failure swings**: equal-ish swing highs/lows left as resting liquidity, e.g. previous day's highs lined up (03:23, 12:49, 25:00).
- **Short-term high/low taken**: the CISD closure itself sweeps a recent swing, "not just the candle next to it" (15:36); more significant when it is buyside/sellside liquidity (13:28).
- **New phase of price**: the shift (reversal/consolidation) that can start once an HTF target is reached (03:38, 28:52).

## Procedure / checklist
1. Identify a POI (FVG, swing, swept low/high) and wait for price to reach it (04:16).
2. Watch for a V-shaped reclaim: closure through the opposing candle series (single candle allowed if only one; ignore non-swing candles) within ~1-3 candles, more if the retracement leg was large (06:06, 09:55, 12:58, 16:42).
3. If the reclaim is slow/sideways or fails, reclassify as consolidation; stop looking at internal closures; wait for a sweep of a range extreme or a breakout confirmed by a new continuation (07:18, 08:17, 11:28).
4. If the closure also takes a short-term high/low or BSL/SSL, wait one more candle / another continuation; the sweep may be the opposite setup forming (02:16, 13:36, 14:55).
5. Check the HTF: if the HTF target is already hit or the HTF candle has expanded 1-3 candles, stand aside; frame entries during HTF wick formation (03:38, 26:12, 28:54).
6. Entry on the closure through, stop at the protected swing, example target 2R (12:06, 27:29).

- Retrace into FVG, cannot close above the down-close candles: becomes consolidation; range extremes become entry POIs.

### Example 5: breakout of a consolidation (11:12-12:47)

### Example 6: closure that takes all left-side highs (12:47-14:16)

## Mistakes and warnings
- Treating any closure over down-close candles as a CISD when price is consolidating; the closure is often the range manipulation (01:25, 06:26).
- Longing after FOMO into an HTF target already hit (03:30, 28:46).
- Entering when the closure itself takes BSL/SSL; that is where the reversal setup forms (13:36).
- Trading internal closures inside a range (09:18).
- Hunting random continuations rather than framing entries on HTF wick formation (28:00).
- Go back through your journal and mark which losses these filters would have avoided (29:01).

## Conflicts and open questions
- Speed rule is "1, 2 maybe 3" candles (09:55), but at 16:42 he allows "a few more candles" when the retracement leg was large; no ratio is given.
- "Short-term high" vs "significant high (BSL/SSL)": he says it can be the adjacent high, yet at 15:36 "not just the candle next to it"; the threshold for which sweeps trigger the wait rule is undefined.
- "Extremely large candle range" for using an inversion as CISD (18:36) has no number.
- "Multiple candles of expansion" on the HTF is given as 1-2-3 hourly candles in the NQ example (28:38); not stated as a general count.
- Entry timeframe and instruments for most examples are not named in speech; frames are too low resolution to read the symbol.
- The page title mentions order blocks, but the video never uses the term "order block".
