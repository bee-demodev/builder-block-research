TTrades presents a short self-made PDF lesson on how much confirmation to demand inside a higher-timeframe Candle 2 reversal: the stronger and cleaner the bias and structure, the less confirmation (reversal entry on the first CISD); the weaker, the more (continuation entry, SMT, or both). ## Mistakes and warnings
- Taking a higher-timeframe reversal that forms midway through the candle. Fix: Only trade reversals that form early in the candle; otherwise wait for the new candle open and a continuation. (37:30)
- Continuation entries that keep getting stopped out because the trader ignores the higher-timeframe candles and enters at the end of a candle. Fix: Wait for the new HTF candle to form its wick and enter on the continuation inside it, using the HTF extreme as stop. (42:20)
- Putting the stop on a high that formed as a consolidation (an untrusted wick). Fix: Ask 'can I trust this high?'; if not, use a wider stop or wait for a continuation that gives a trustworthy high. (51:20)
- Shorting in the lower half of the swing-to-target range. Fix: Short only in the upper 50% between the swing and the first target. (26:40)
- Getting chopped trading London reversals on 1-3m before the move. Fix: Use 15m/30m; the open drives price; invalidation is the low or a continuation. (1:28:40)
- Entering in 'macro' time windows. Fix: Wait for the candle open (e.g. 10 a.m.), let the wick form, then trade. (1:23:30)
- Chasing very high R:R (e.g. 17R) examples. Fix: 2R is enough. (1:00:00)
