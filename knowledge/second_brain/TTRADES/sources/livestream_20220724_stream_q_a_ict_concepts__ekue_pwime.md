## Q&A
- **Q (10:00):** Were you profitable before starting ICT? **A:** No; he had moments but was not profitable.
- **Q (11:40):** Can we look at RUT (Russell)? **A:** On the daily it touched a small order block; liquidity was taken, then a move higher, consolidation and a breakout. He never really looks at the Russell but expects it to go the same way as the other indices.
- **Q (12:20):** DXY read? **A:** Bullish: price continuously supported by down-close candles and the down moves are not aggressive. Target the weekly FVG fill then a little higher into a daily rebalance.
- **Q (13:20):** Do you trade solely futures or options as well? **A:** Sometimes SPY options, but he mostly stays away because options are bad for his emotions.
- **Q (14:30):** EURUSD read; aren't DXY and EURUSD inversely related? **A:** He doesn't do forex but agrees; read is the inverse of DXY: reach up into the gap before continuing lower, two-day FVG below. If bullish you'd want an aggressive move up, then down.
- **Q (16:40):** Can you explain multi-timeframe analysis / how we'd know from this? **A:** He watches 15m, 5m and 1m intraday for structure. Example ES Friday 22 Jul: liquidity run on 15m, aggressive breaks of structure on 1m and 5m shift lower-timeframe structure, while on 15m it is still technically a pullback; with the HTF reason (liquidity taken, bias lower) it makes more sense for it to drop. Calls it 'telescoping'.
- **Q (17:50):** How do we know if a market structure shift will happen? **A:** You don't know for sure that a lower-timeframe MSS will work out on higher timeframes; what you know is that you have a higher-timeframe reason to be short, you ran liquidity and got an aggressive move. Focus on speed and distance covered.
- **Q (18:50):** (Joseph) Struggling with changing timeframes **A:** If starting out, only look at the 15-minute and follow ICT's 2022 mentorship model: at 8:30 look left for where liquidity rests; targets are equal lows, lows and FVGs. Wait for a 15m run of stops, switch to the 5m for a structure shift with an FVG, use the 1m FVG if the 5m FVG is in discount when shorting.
- **Q (22:10):** Explain the importance of order blocks and why they occur **A:** An order block is where people load their position before the move (e.g. the up-close candle, or just its body, before a move down); if the fast move leaves orders unfilled, price comes back to fill them. He doesn't use them much — focuses on liquidity and imbalances — but uses them to narrow down inside a large imbalance on the 1m, or when there is a single order block before a large move.
- **Q (24:10):** If there are multiple fair value gaps, which do you choose? **A:** Depends on risk tolerance and R:R. You are only technically invalidated when the low (the liquidity) is taken, so entering the higher FVG means a wider stop; take a lighter position so you risk the same amount of your account.
- **Q (25:00):** Don't you think NQ will take out all the liquidity above those highs? **A:** Possible, and if it does it creates an SMT divergence, but he wants some retracement before going higher, unless news rips it.
- **Q (26:00):** How to take short 1-minute scalps with a 5-minute bias? **A:** In a trending market use market structure: in a downtrend any up-close candle or imbalance can be risked; enter at 50% of the displacement range; go imbalance to imbalance — scalp the imbalance, risk the low of the imbalance, target the liquidity/next imbalance. In an uptrend imbalances support price; after a structure shift up-close candles resist. He no longer does this himself.
- **Q (27:50):** How do you find your daily bias? **A:** Imbalances and liquidity; if nothing else, mark out liquidity. Showed an hourly Twitter markup: liquidity taken, expected a retracement, not exactly right; the same concepts apply to the daily.
- **Q (30:30):** (Pete) SMT divergences? **A:** Correlated indices like ES and NQ should track each other's structure; when one breaks a short-term low and the other fails, that divergence signals a potential reversal and the one that failed is stronger. He prefers to look for them on the 15-minute.
- **Q (33:00):** How do you play SMT? **A:** At the divergence area go to a lower timeframe (5m/1m), watch structure for a setup (higher high, higher low, displacement) and take a confirmation entry; or enter at the divergence and risk where you're invalidated, which can be a large stop (~10 ES points). Time matters: 'time and price, not price and time'.
- **Q (34:10):** Which symbol to trade on a divergence (the one that held or the one that swept)? **A:** Two views: the one that didn't make the low is stronger, or the one that took liquidity is accumulating for a stronger move. Experiment yourself. Key is where you're invalidated: on the one that held, its low; on the one that swept, arguably when the other symbol breaks its low.
- **Q (35:00):** How can you trade this model if you can't look at anything below the 15/16-minute? **A:** Price action is fractal: use daily/4H/1H instead of 15/5/1; setups appear on the hourly but take longer. Find POIs on higher timeframes, set alerts, look for lower-timeframe setups. Showed inducement (IDM: equal lows run) plus an FVG as a long entry.
- **Q (36:30):** Confirmation for entries? **A:** Uses the 1m or sometimes 15s. After liquidity is run he could enter immediately, but prefers waiting for a break/shift of structure, then takes the displacement range and finds a setup in its upper half (for a short). Friday's ES short also had inducement; AMTrades took it.
- **Q (38:50):** How do you spot lower highs and higher lows? **A:** Three-candle pattern: a candle with a high, a candle with a higher high, then a candle with a lower high = the swing high (and opposite for lows). Don't overthink; a line chart shows structure simply. A run of liquidity with no displacement is not a market structure shift.
- **Q (42:30):** CL (oil) read? **A:** Still bearish: up-close candles / FVGs resisting price; targets 93 and 90.57. If bullish he'd want lows run (done) and then an aggressive move up.
- **Q (45:20):** Breaker example? **A:** A breaker is an order block that price went through and then comes back to: e.g. a small down-close candle that should support price; price goes above, takes liquidity, breaks down aggressively through it, and it then acts as resistance. He doesn't use breakers much; mostly FVGs and liquidity.
- **Q (48:00):** Which ICT video should I start with? **A:** Go through ICT's whole 2022 mentorship, at least the first ~20-25 videos, and rewatch them.

## Mistakes and warnings
- Shorting from an FVG that sits in a discount of the range Fix: Drop to the 1m and find an FVG in premium (20:20)
- Calling a liquidity run with no displacement a market structure shift Fix: Require an energetic move through structure (speed and distance) (39:40)
- Getting faked out by a 1m structure shift that is only a pullback on the 15m Fix: Check the higher timeframe; balance early entries against confirmation entries (41:10)
- Entering an SMT reversal blindly at the divergence Fix: Drop to 5m/1m and wait for structure plus displacement at a good time; otherwise the stop is large (~10 ES points) (33:20)
- Beginners juggling too many timeframes Fix: Only use the 15m to start, following ICT's 2022 mentorship model (18:50)
- Over-scalping imbalances Fix: He stopped scalping because he does better waiting for good trades (27:10)
- Trading options Fix: He avoids them because they are bad for his emotions (13:20)
