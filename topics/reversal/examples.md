# Reversal Examples

## Previous-Day High Sweep -> Bearish Reversal
- Price expands into the previous-day high during a key session.
- Higher timeframe leaves an upper wick and closes back through the swept level or back inside the source range.
- Lower timeframe prints CISD and the first protected high in the new bearish direction.
- Initial objective is previous-day equilibrium, then the opposite side of the range.

## Previous-Day Low Sweep -> Bullish Reversal
- Price runs sell-side liquidity below a previous-day or session low.
- Candle closes back above the swept level, creating a wick-based rejection.
- Lower timeframe confirms with opposite-direction displacement and protected swing formation.
- Continuation after the reversal is preferred over forcing the first touch blindly.

## Candle 3 Reversal Confirmation
- Candle 2 reaches an already-mapped fair value gap, imbalance, or order-block style zone but fails to give the needed reversal closure.
- Candle 3 closes through candle 2's body without sweeping candle 2's extreme.
- Candle 3 equilibrium is then used to refine candle 4 expansion in the new direction.

## Reversal Sequence Entry
- Purge or turtle soup sweeps liquidity during London or New York at a mapped higher-timeframe reversal anchor.
- Price reclaims the extreme area, then prints CISD.
- A fair value gap, candle-three equilibrium, or breaker-style retest offers the actual entry.
- Later confirmation is cleaner but usually gives a worse fill.
